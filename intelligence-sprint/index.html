The Intelligence Sprint

Programme design · Keithan Cokbilir

Brief. Engineers on driver-assistance and vehicle-software teams know things about how their systems behave in real conditions that product management never hears — or hears too late, filtered through formal reviews.

I designed a 4-week structured sprint that helps engineers turn a technical observation into a brief product management can act on, with a 6-week follow-up arc for the strongest insight.

My role: audience analysis, sprint architecture, insight-brief template, engineering-to-commercial job aid, baseline and day-30 measurement design.
Status
Concept project — fully designed, not yet delivered. Everything below is the complete design and its working artefacts.
Key design decisions
  1. A five-part template instead of free-form submissionsWithout structure, week-2 submissions vary too much for non-engineers to compare. The template forces each insight to link an observation to a live programme, an implication and supporting data.
  2. The data job aid arrives in week 3, not week 1Engineers get the one-page "technical metric → business argument" aid only once they have a real insight that needs it — learning at the point of need.
  3. A story-based baseline questionAlongside two confidence scales, I ask "When did a technical observation last change a product decision?" — a qualitative baseline that is harder to game than self-ratings.

01

Insight brief template

The most important single artefact in the Intelligence Sprint. A five-section structured template that engineers complete in week 2 — anchoring their insight to a live programme, describing the technical observation, translating it into a commercial or regulatory implication, and supporting it with real data. Without this template, week 2 produces inconsistent submissions that product management cannot evaluate.

02

4-week sprint structure

Week 1 anchors each insight to a live engineering programme. Week 2 is the submission and peer review moment. Week 3 releases a one-page engineering data job aid — three views that translate technical metrics into a commercial argument — at the moment when teams have a real question to answer. Week 4 is the review day with product management.
Open sprint brief →
03

Baseline measurement — engineering version

Three questions specific to an engineering audience. Question 1 measures commercial translation confidence — the primary skill gap. Question 2 measures belief that insights reach product management — the environment gap. Question 3 is the most powerful: "when was the last time a technical observation led to a commercial decision?" Most engineers will answer "I can't think of one." That answer becomes the qualitative baseline the day-30 result is compared against.

Measurement & completion pack

What this pack closes
Insight brief template — the structured format that makes week 2 submissions evaluable by product management. A short note on how the briefs can also serve as AI-literacy documentation in regulated teams.
The three artefacts most likely to determine whether the programme produces a real case study — not what participants experience during four weeks, but what was measured before it started and how submissions are structured.

"The knowledge already exists. The design builds the pathway to surface it."

The Intelligence Sprint is not designed to teach engineers about commercial thinking. It is designed to surface what they already know and create a structured pathway for the best insights to reach product management with a brief they can act on. If no insights enter the development pathway, the design goes back to the action map.
